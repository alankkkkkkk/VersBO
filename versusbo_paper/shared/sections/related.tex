\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{LLMs in Bayesian optimization.}
Representation methods fit a GP on LLM embeddings of reaction text, either frozen \citep{rankovic2023bochemian} or finetuned, with a Laplace approximation \citep{kristiadi2024sober} or through the GP marginal likelihood as in GOLLuM \citep{rankovic2026gollum,sin2026dynamic}; their GP input has 768 to 4096 dimensions. Surrogate and optimizer methods let the LLM predict outcomes or propose experiments: LLAMBO \citep{liu2024llambo}, BO-ICL \citep{ramos2026boicl}, LLM-GO \citep{macknight2025pretrained}, and BORA and Reasoning BO, which interleave LLM reasoning with a GP loop \citep{cisse2025bora,yang2026reasoningbo}. Prior methods shape the GP with LLM beliefs: LGBO shifts the GP mean toward regions the LLM prefers \citep{yuan2026lgbo}, CALIPER gates each LLM prior by its observed error \citep{chen2026caliper}, LABO treats LLM predictions as a cheap fidelity \citep{chen2026labo}, and LLM-Pref elicits a utility from LLM preferences between reaction conditions \citep{patel2026distilling}. ReElicit has an LLM define interpretable features \citep{lin2026embedding}, and CAKE evolves GP kernels with an LLM \citep{suwandi2025cake}. \ours{} asks about properties of reagents rather than outcomes, and every answer enters a likelihood whose reliability is learned from the experiments.

\paragraph{Categorical BO.}
Latent-variable GPs map each level of a categorical factor to a learned point in a low-dimensional space \citep{zhang2020latent,oune2021latent}; with few observations per level, the regime of an experimental campaign, these points are poorly identified, and \ours{} adds a second likelihood that identifies them. Tabular foundation models serve as zero-shot BO surrogates \citep{hollmann2025tabpfn,rogers2026zeroshot}.

\paragraph{Paired comparisons.}
Thurstone and Bradley--Terry models explain paired comparisons through latent scale values \citep{thurstone1927law,bradley1952rank}. Preference GPs and preferential BO learn a latent utility from duels \citep{chu2005preference,gonzalez2017preferential}, and LILO converts language feedback into preferences \citep{kobalczyk2026lilo}. LLM judges rank text more consistently with pairwise prompts than with absolute scores \citep{liusie2024comparative,qin2024prp,zheng2023judging}, though their answers depend on option order \citep{wang2024fair}. These methods compare outcomes; \ours{} compares reagents along named properties, asks in both orders, and uses the answers to shape the input space of the GP.
