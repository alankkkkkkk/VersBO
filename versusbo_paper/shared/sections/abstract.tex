Bayesian optimization (BO) of chemical reactions spends most of its budget on categorical choices: which ligand, which base, which solvent. Large language models (LLMs) know a great deal about these reagents, yet current methods ask them for absolute quantities such as embeddings, scores or yield predictions, which are hard to calibrate and expensive to query. We measure a simpler judgment. Across five LLMs and four reaction datasets with DFT descriptors, a model orders two reagents along a named property, such as base strength, in agreement with the descriptor for \PairAccPct{} of pairs, while orderings implied by the same model's 1--10 ratings agree for \RateAccPct{}. We introduce \ours{}, which treats these pairwise comparisons as noisy evidence about named latent coordinates of each reagent. A Gaussian process models the outcome on these coordinates plus a few unnamed free dimensions, an ordered-probit likelihood with a learned reliability per axis sets how much each kind of LLM judgment counts, and \ours{} asks only the comparisons whose answers could change the next experiment. With every reliability at zero it reduces to a latent-variable GP. On nine reaction optimization tasks, \ours{} reaches the lowest final regret on seven and finds a top-5\% experiment in \IReduction{} fewer iterations than the fastest baseline. When the LLM's chemistry is made wrong, \ours{} ends within \CfGapPct{} of a knowledge-free GP, and it uses \TokLLMGO{} times fewer tokens than an LLM-driven optimizer.
